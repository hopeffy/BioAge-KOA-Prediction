SINGLE-FEATURE BA -> KOA ACROSS ALL COHORTS
================================================================================
Date: 2026-07-10

PURPOSE
----------------------------------------
Address advisor feedback #4: show BA as a single feature predicting KOA
across all cohorts. Provides a direct head-to-head comparison of every
BA variant's standalone KOA predictive value, without confounding from
other features in the model.

INTERPRETATION
----------------------------------------

1. ALL BA VARIANTS ARE WEAK STANDALONE PREDICTORS IN CHARLS

   Every BA variant — Sheet1's pre-computed BA, our KDM-BA, PhenoAge, and
   BIR — achieves only 0.50-0.54 AUROC as a single predictor of KOA. This
   is barely above chance (0.50). The best (Sheet1 BA at 0.536) explains
   almost no KOA variance on its own.

   This refutes any interpretation that BA is a strong KOA predictor.
   BA's contribution in the raw17 model (AUROC 0.896) comes from
   interaction effects with other features, not from standalone
   predictive power. When SHAP ranks BA #1 with importance 0.439 in
   the full model, that importance reflects conditional/interaction
   effects — not marginal predictive value.

2. ML-PREDICTED BA HAS MODESTLY MORE SIGNAL EXTERNALLY THAN IN CHARLS

   ML_KDM_BA gets 0.589 in ELSA and 0.629 in HRS as a single feature —
   higher than any BA variant in CHARLS (max 0.536). But this is
   misleading: HRS has 33.9% KOA prevalence (vs CHARLS 13.3%), and
   Age_New alone gets 0.599 in HRS. The apparent improvement is driven
   by prevalence and age-group distribution, not by BA quality.

3. ML_KDM_BA ADDS MORE OVER AGE ALONE THAN ML_BA

   The incremental value of BA over Age_New (the no-BA baseline):

     ELSA: ML_KDM_BA vs Age_New  = +0.036
     ELSA: ML_BA    vs Age_New   = +0.003
     HRS:  ML_KDM_BA vs Age_New  = +0.030
     HRS:  ML_BA    vs Age_New   = +0.030

   ML_KDM_BA (predicted from KDM-BA target) adds more KOA signal beyond
   age than ML_BA (predicted from Sheet1 BA target). This may be because
   KDM-BA is computed from inflammation/metabolic biomarkers directly
   relevant to osteoarthritis pathology, while Sheet1 BA's formula is
   unknown and may encode less KOA-relevant signal.

4. CROSS-POPULATION BA-ILLNESS RELATIONSHIP IS UNSTABLE

   In CHARLS, all BA variants have near-zero KOA correlation (r=0.02-0.04).
   In HRS, ML-predicted BA gets AUROC 0.629 — but this is mostly the age
   effect (Age_New alone = 0.599). The BA increment over age is small
   (+0.03) and inconsistent across BA methods.

   This supports the manuscript's central argument: the biomarker-BA-KOA
   relationship established in CHARLS does not transport reliably to other
   populations. Even when we predict BA from available demographics and
   apply it externally, the BA-illness link is weak and unstable.

5. IMPLICATION FOR THE NARRATIVE CHAIN

   Single-feature results confirm that BA is not a magic variable. Its
   value is context-dependent (interaction with other features in the
   full model) and population-specific (computed from CHARLS biomarkers
   with CHARLS-specific biomarker-illness relationships). Removing BA
   from the model costs 0.037 AUROC (step_18), but replacing it with
   our transparent KDM-BA costs 0.065 — suggesting interaction effects
   matter more than the BA formula itself.

   The cross-cultural gap (0.859 CHARLS raw16 -> 0.604 ELSA/HRS raw16)
   dwarfs all BA-related effects combined (0.037-0.065 AUROC).
